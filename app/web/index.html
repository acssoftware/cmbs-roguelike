About

This is a project by a decade-long, passionate member of the Combat Mission community, one who keeps craving more content around this amazing work of art that is the Combat Mission series.

Check out any updates on the app on my YouTube channel.

I hope this mod makes you enjoy Combat Mission even more.

Why Node.js?

Before there was an app, there were months of reverse engineering of the game's files: how a battle, a campaign and a save are packed, compressed and read back. That work was written in JavaScript, and every piece of it was proven inside the game, often after long sessions of trial and error. Node.js runs that same code unchanged, so the app is built directly on what was already proven to work, instead of rewriting it and testing it all over again in the game.

It also keeps things simple for you:

  • Nothing to install. The app carries its own copy of Node.js (about 30 MB) that runs from its folder, with no installer and no administrator rights.
  • No extra packages. The app's server uses only what comes with Node.js: nothing is downloaded from the internet.
  • Your browser is the window. The screens are plain web pages served by the app on your own computer (only to it, never to the network). The app opens them in a window of its own through Microsoft Edge, which every Windows 10/11 has, or in your default browser.

The other options weighed less well. An old-style Windows HTML application (HTA) runs on the retired Internet Explorer engine, too slow to open a 16 MB save, and would have meant rewriting everything. Electron would wrap the same Node.js in about 200 MB more. C# or Python would have meant a full rewrite, or an installation.

Campaigns

Setup

The app writes the campaign file into the game's Campaigns folder and reads the transition save from Saved Games. Nothing else in the game folder is touched, and no file is ever overwritten. Undoing a battle deletes the saves the app wrote for it — never one the game wrote.

Your campaigns

Your settings and every campaign the app makes are kept in this folder.

System check

Environment

System check

Before the first campaign, the app makes sure this computer has what it needs: the game folder, permission to write battles into it, the game version, and the app's own files. Nothing is installed or changed, except the game's Campaigns and Saved Games folders, created if they are missing.

New campaign

These hold for the whole campaign. None of them can be changed once it is raised — the battles behind and the battles ahead have to be the same game.

Difficulty ?Sets every parameter below in one go. Change any of them and the campaign becomes Custom; put them back and it is the preset again.

Your library ?The armies you may buy from, now and between battles. Pick more than one and your force can mix them — the battle file declares one nationality, and the app writes whichever army most of your force belongs to. Nothing stops you putting the enemy's army in here and fighting your own: the game has two sides, not three allegiances.

The enemy ?Which side holds the ground you attack. You are the attacker in every battle. Where the side has two armies the defending one is drawn fresh each battle, so a campaign against the American side really does put Ukrainians across the line some of the time. The enemy is generated from scratch for every battle and carries nothing forward — only your force keeps its dead.

Force ratio ?Your force against the enemy's in an ordinary battle: at 1.3 : 1 you field 1.3 times what the enemy fields. Your own credits do not change with it; only the enemy grows or shrinks. A Meeting Engagement is 1 : 1 at every level. A player who loses nothing stays at this ratio the whole campaign; losses are covered only in part. Lower is harder.

Rescued soldiers ?A man who is wounded rather than killed was carried off the field. This is his chance of being back with his squad by the next battle at no cost; the rest you pay for at the quartermaster like any other replacement. The roll is seeded — campaign, battle and the man's place in the roster — so reading the same result twice gives the same men back, and the screen can promise before you pay exactly what the writing will do.

Operational points ?The campaign is a branching path of battles, and what waits in each one is hidden: the map, and the enemy. Operational points buy a look before you choose — the map of a battle, or the whole enemy order of battle in the Intel (the Rules page has the prices). Some battles pay points back when you do not lose them. This is how many you start with.

Defeats ?How many battles you can lose before the campaign is lost. The next defeat after the last one you can afford ends it. In Ironman, the first defeat ends the campaign; unlimited, no defeat does — the campaign always runs to its last battle.

Special forces ?What a special forces formation costs in this campaign. A campaign fields at most one, and every unit of it is a veteran whatever the price. Free matches no difficulty preset: the campaign becomes Custom and its score multiplier drops.
